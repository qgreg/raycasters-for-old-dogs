A standing-still guide to pointing at things
Checking your headset…

The whole trick

  1. Hold a controller in each hand. A beam of light shines out of the front.
  2. Move your hand to aim the beam — not your head, not your eyes.
  3. When the dot lands on a target, squeeze the trigger with your index finger.
  4. That is it. Every button, menu, and door in virtual reality works this way.

Things worth knowing

  • 🦴
    Treat

    Aim with your whole arm, not your wrist. It is steadier, and it does not tire out.

  • 🥏
    Fetch

    Ten short lessons, then a practice range that keeps going as long as you want it to. The controller comes first: trigger, grip, the thumbstick in all four directions and pressed down, and both round buttons — on both hands, one control at a time. Pointing comes after, once your hands already know where everything is.

  • 🖐️
    Watch your hands

    A picture of each controller floats beside you, one per hand. The part being asked for glows; the part you are pressing right now lights up; the parts you have proved work turn green and stay green. The thumbstick picture has a dot that moves exactly as the real stick moves, so you can see it working.

  • 🚪
    The way out

    Two buttons belong to the headset, not to this page: Meta on the right and Menu (marked with three little lines) on the left. Pressing Meta makes everything vanish — that is not a mistake, and pressing it again brings you back. Lesson 5 has you do both on purpose, so neither ever comes as a surprise. If pressing it drops you back to this page, just press start again — you carry on exactly where you left off.

  • 🪢
    Leash

    You stay standing in one spot the whole time. Nothing moves you, so there is nothing here that can make you queasy.

  • ⚙️
    Under the hood

    A board on your right shows live controller readings. It is for the curious — ignoring it costs you nothing.

For whoever is setting this up
  • Open this page in the Meta Quest Browser app, then press the start button above.
  • The headset asks permission to enter immersive mode — say yes.
  • Hand tracking works too. Put the controllers down and pinch thumb to index finger instead of squeezing a trigger.
  • Buttons float below: Repeat, Skip, Hands, and Board. They are pointed at and clicked exactly like the targets, which is the point.
  • The controller lessons come first and ask for every control on both controllers, alternating hands, so a dead button shows up as the one part of the picture that never turns green.
  • Each thumbstick direction is checked separately. A stick that only travels one way will not pass — which is the point.
  • The pointing lessons start at lesson 6. Lesson 6 finishes on a steady hold rather than a button press; if holding still is the hard part, that timer is in src/lessons.js.
  • The hand pictures and the diagnostics board share the space either side, so they take turns — the Hands and Board buttons swap between them.
  • The Meta button cannot be read by any web page — the headset keeps it. It is verified instead by watching the session get taken away and handed back, which is the counter marked trips away on the board.
  • The Menu button is not in the WebXR controller profile either, so lesson 5 measures rather than assumes: it watches for a session blur or for a button index we do not recognise, and reports honestly whether anything arrived. On most headsets nothing does, and the lesson says so.
  • Pressing Meta may blur the session or end it outright, depending on the headset. Both count as the same journey, so leaving and coming back through the start button advances the lesson either way. And if neither happens, the lesson moves on by itself after twenty seconds rather than waiting for something that may never arrive.
  • The board reports the controller layout exactly as the headset names it, and lists any button it reports that we did not expect. If a headset turns up with a different arrangement, it says so rather than quietly missing it.
